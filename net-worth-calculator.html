What net worth tells you

Net worth is a snapshot of your financial position at a point in time. Add the current value of your assets, subtract the balances you owe, and the difference is your net worth. It can be positive or negative, and neither result is a judgment about you.

What to include

Assets can include cash and savings, investments and retirement accounts, an estimated real-estate value, vehicles, and other meaningful property. Liabilities include debts such as a mortgage, auto loan, student loan, credit-card balance, or other debt.

Use reasonable current values rather than what you originally paid. For assets whose value changes frequently, the result will naturally move over time. Debt balances also change as payments are made and interest accrues.

How often should you check?

There is no required schedule. Some people use a monthly or quarterly snapshot to understand direction over time. The useful question is usually not whether one number is “good,” but how the relationship between assets and liabilities is changing.
